Speed to lead

Why the first five minutes after a lead comes in matter most

When someone fills out your form or sends you a message, they are thinking about your business right now. An hour later, they're back to their day, and they may have already messaged someone else.

Research backs this up. According to a roundup of sales follow-up studies from ZoomInfo, responding within one hour makes you about seven times more likely to qualify a lead than waiting 24 hours.

You don't need to be glued to your phone to be fast. Turn on notifications for new leads, keep a saved reply ready, and send a quick "Got your message, I'll call you at 3" if you can't talk yet. A fast, short reply beats a perfect one that comes tomorrow.

Customer retention

The barber problem: getting customers to come back

Think about the last time you got a great haircut. Did the barber ever reach out to see if you were ready for the next one? For most people, the answer is no, and that's the barber problem.

A great first visit isn't enough on its own. Customers get busy, forget, or end up somewhere else simply because it was closer that day. The fix is a simple reminder system: book the next visit before they leave, and send a friendly text a few days before they're usually due.

This works for any business with repeat customers, from lawn care to lash techs. Write down how often each customer usually comes back, and reach out right before that time, not after.

Mindset

Following up is not being pushy

A lot of people stop after one message because they don't want to be annoying. But the same ZoomInfo roundup found that only about 12% of salespeople make three or more follow-up attempts, even though most sales take five or more.

Following up becomes pushy when it's only about what you want. It becomes helpful when every message gives the other person something: an answer, a reminder, a better time, or an easy way to say no.

Before you hit send, ask yourself one question: "Would I be glad to get this message?" If the answer is yes, send it. If not, add something useful first.
